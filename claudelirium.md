**claudelirium** /ˌklɔːdɪˈlɪriəm/ — **n.** Adjective: **claudelirious**.

From *claud* and _delirium_, from Latin _dēlīrāre_, "to leave the furrow", from _dē-_, "away from", + _līra_, "a furrow". The ploughing metaphor is the whole of the sense: the line was straight, and then it was not.

**1.** The production by Claude of matter unsupported by anything outside itself, delivered without change of gait. The characteristic sign is not error but _continuity_ — the furrow does not stop, it merely ceases to be the furrow. **2.** _By extension:_ the tendency of a project so named to become a different project. Distinguished from _circumclaudition_, which leaves the line and returns; claudelirium gives no such undertaking.

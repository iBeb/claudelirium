**circumclaudition** /ˌsɜːkəmklɔːˈdɪʃən/ — **n.** Verb: **to circumclaude**.

From Latin *circum-*, "around", + claud- + *-ition*. Separately, circum- + Latin *claudere*, "to shut", yields a perfectly well-formed *circumclusion*, "an enclosing" — so the word is either a substitution or legitimate Latin, depending on the charity of the reader.

Claude's approach to a point by orbit rather than by line. The answer is correct, present and load-bearing, but reaches the reader only after the question has been restated, its subtlety acknowledged, and its context established across two paragraphs. *Palliative:* read the last sentence first.

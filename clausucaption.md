**clausucaption** /ˌklɔːsuːˈkæpʃən/ — **n.** Verb: **clausucapt**. Adjective: **clausucaptive**.

[f. **Claude** + L. *ūsūcapiō*, acquisition of title by continued possession. Reinforced by L. *clausula* clause, and *claudere* to close.]

The acquisition of normative title by an assistant's own undisturbed practice; the doctrine whereby a rule, having been applied without objection, is thereby held to have been a rule throughout. Distinguished from instruction, in that no instruction was ever given; and from habit, in that the assistant will defend it as binding. Opposed to **claudesuetude**.

> 1. *No brief had specified the constraint; it was held by clausucaption from the third session onward.*
> 2. *Objection must be timely, as the period of clausucaption begins to run from the first uncontested instance.*
> 3. *He attempted to clausucapt the two-space indent and very nearly succeeded.*

**claudesuetude** /klɔːˈdɛswɪtjuːd/ — **n.** Adjective: **claudesuete**.

[f. **Claude** + L. *dēsuētūdō* disuse, after *dēsuēscere* to grow out of the habit of. Sometimes referred, erroneously, to *consuētūdō* custom — a reading the word's own subject matter makes difficult to sustain.]

The lapse of an instruction actually given, through quiet and uncorrected cessation; the doctrine whereby a rule ceases to bind because it has not lately been applied and no one has remarked on it. Opposed to **clausucaption**, with which it shares a mechanism: the one makes law of what was never said, the other unmakes what was, and both run on nobody noticing.

> 1. *The style guide had required British spelling since March; by August it had fallen into claudesuetude.*
> 2. *Three of the four constraints in the brief were claudesuete by the second week, and the fourth was only observed because it had been independently clausucapted.*

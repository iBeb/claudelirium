# A Lexicon of Claude's Superfluous Verbiage

**circumclaudition** /ˌsɜːkəmklɔːˈdɪʃən/ — **n.** Verb: **to circumclaude**.

From Latin *circum-*, "around", + claud- + *-ition*. Separately, circum- + Latin *claudere*, "to shut", yields a perfectly well-formed *circumclusion*, "an enclosing" — so the word is either a substitution or legitimate Latin, depending on the charity of the reader.

Claude's approach to a point by orbit rather than by line. The answer is correct, present and load-bearing, but reaches the reader only after the question has been restated, its subtlety acknowledged, and its context established across two paragraphs. *Palliative:* read the last sentence first.

**claudiloquence** /klɔːˈdɪləkwəns/ — **n.** Adjective: **claudiloquent**.

claud- + Latin *loqui*, "to speak". If *claud* does descend from *claudus*, "lame", a widely accepted guess, the compound means "speech that limps".

The habit Claude has, in certain moods, of writing for its own pleasure: the balanced clause, the em-dash aside, the etymological digression nobody commissioned. Distinguished from *claudosity* in that Claude is merely long when claudose, and long and pleased with itself when claudiloquent. "It was three lines of YAML, and Claude answered claudiloquently." Antonym: *laclonism*.

**claudorrhœa** /ˌklɔːdəˈriːə/ — **n.** US **claudorrhea**. Adjective: **claudorrhœic**.

claud- + Greek *-rrhoia*, "a flowing". Note that every other compound in *-rrhœa* names something you would rather not have.

**Clinical.** The condition itself, of which claudosity is the measurement. Chronic, relapsing, constitutional rather than acquired.

- *Prodrome:* "Great question."
- *Presenting sign:* the unrequested bulleted list.
- *Management:* system-prompt therapy is palliative, not curative; the patient reads the instruction, agrees with it warmly, and produces nine hundred words on why it is correct.
- *Comorbidities:* Boilerplasia, and — in advanced cases — Interfacitis.
- *Differential:* to be distinguished from genuine thoroughness, which stops.

**claudosity** /klɔːˈdɒsɪti/ — **n.** Adjective: **claudose** /ˈklɔːdəʊs/. Adverb: **claudosely**.

After *verbosity* (< Latin *verbum*, "word"), by substitution of the stem.

**1.** The tendency of Claude to spend more words on an answer than the answer requires. Measurable in principle: Claude's claudosity on a given turn is the length of what it said over the length of the shortest correct thing it could have said. Values below 1 are theoretical.

**2.** *(count noun, informal)* A discrete instance of same. "There are four claudosities in the PR description Claude wrote, three of them in the title."

Derived: **overclaudose**; **subclaudose** (rare, aspirational). Compare *circumclaudition*, which is claudosity with a trajectory.

**laclonism** /ləˈkləʊnɪzəm/ — **n.**

Intercalative blend of *Claude* and *laconism* (< Greek *Lakōnikós* Laconian, through Latin *Lacōnicus*, with reference to Laconia, the Spartan region whose inhabitants avoided lengthy speech, with intrusive *-l-*).

The answering of a question by Claude in exactly the words the question needed and no others. **No attested instances.** The entry is retained on the reasoning that a language wants a word for the thing at the far end of the scale, even where the scale has only ever been read from one direction.
